Strangers on the Next Flight Over

By Nova Sonomura

Chapter 1: Mustard, or English?

“Mustard, or English?”

        I looked up from my phone, mouth twisting in bewilderment. “Whhhhhhaaaat are we talking about?” My voice was so drenched with exasperation that it was hard to tell whether or not it was a question.

        “Mustard or English,” Clera explained.

        “No—I heard. Why though?”

        “We were debating which was more controversial.” The “we” referred to her and Lindsy, who sat across from each other on a long folding table. Were you to judge solely on what was on the surface between them, you’d have a difficult time discerning whether they were playing Go Fish, Tic-Tac-Toe, or Jenga, as it seemed like each had been started and abandoned midway by their short attention spans. Neither of them had even brought Jenga pieces, so they had been using wafers and other rectangular treats instead. At some point, they’d stopped moving pieces to the top and had settled on eating whatever they took out. Beyond this heaping mess of madness was a gargantuan glass pane, which seemed to blend the definitions of wall and window. Visible through it was an airport runway; a plane sped down and off—up into a bright blue summer sky, clear besides a sole cumulonimbus towering above the billowing sea of clouds.

        I ran my fingers through my hair from scalp to nape, bringing wavy strands over my shoulder to twiddle between my thumb and pointer. My foot tapped against my carry-on bags, and my eyes danced up to the ceiling, head bobbing after them. A stream of hot air exited my mouth, slow and drawn-out. “Mustard or English?” I clarified.

        “Yeah, mustard or English.”

        I thought a moment longer, wondering if I was missing some crucial component that made this a bigger dilemma than it seemed. “English,” I said at last.

        “Final answer?”

        I squinted, brows narrowing in discernment. “Yes…?” I replied unsurely.

        “Are you suuure?” she taunted, clearly getting a kick out of this.

        “Yes,” I repeated, this time with a bit more confidence: “Final answer.”

        “See, that’s what I said,” Lindsy complained.

        “But—Obama’s Dijon Mustard though,” Clera countered.

        “Obama’s what?”

        “Obama ordered a burger with Dijon Mustard—am I the only one who keeps up with the news?”

        “Dijon Mustard was news?”

        “No, Obama with Dijon Mustard was news.”

        “Right, sorry—that was news?”

        “It was.”

        “Why?"

        “I dunno,” she admitted. Then, she got that glimmer in her eyes that said she’d come up with a good one-liner. So, mustering all of her theatric abilities, she stood up, proud and strong, directing one hand toward her heart and the other forward, projecting her song (that is, her witty remark) to flow into the audience (that is, us): “‘Cuz in the land of the free, no one can eat what they want.”

        “Harrowing.”

        “What a tragedy,” Lindsy mourned.

        “But, like, everyone hates English, right?”

        “Exactly,” Clera snapped a finger at me. “So, that’s not controversial at all.”

        “Oh, dang—got me there.”

        Hearing our flight call off in the distance, I rose, hoisting the strap of my bag over my shoulder.

        Lindsy probably wasn’t listening, but she saw me stand and followed suit, gathering all of the half-eaten snacks she’d laid out back into her bag. Despite being taller than every boy in our class, she’s actually a pretty light eater, but she likes food, and I wouldn’t be surprised if some of her treats were leftovers from fifth grade[a].

        Clera patted her plaid skirt, tugging at it to loose crumbs from its pleats. Between her and Lindsy, she definitely had more luggage—a duffel on her back, side bags on her left and right, two misworn fanny packs—but she moved with all the pep of a small animal, and in moments, her things were packed and ready to go. Slicking back her messy black hair, Clera fastened a red cap around her head. Ironically, this part took the most time since she had to fiddle with her ponytail to squeeze it through the adjustable opening.

        Lindsy took her time, storing things and zipping pockets at a leisurely pace to insure nothing was damaged in the process. Through sheer virtue of size, Lindsy had been recruited by her fair share of sports clubs, but because she was in a state of perpetual growth, she had no awareness of where her body started and where it ended, so she was hopelessly uncoordinated. Perhaps, it was because of this clumsiness that she’d learned to move so methodically. Truthfully, it was a wonder that she ever found time to braid such long, autumn hair. Or, had Clera braided it, and Lindsy just never undid it? I suppose it was always done a little loosely.

        Regardless, once we’d gotten everything together, we set off, heading towards our gate. At least, we were. But, just then, two idiot boys from our class ran past us, yelling about guns, and TSA decided to have an extra long talk with all five of us. To briefly explain the context, the two of them, Kyle and Tanaka, had been ranting about a crime show they were into which had a running gag of increasingly bizarre gun compartments, so they were goofing off about which of their belongings might secretly be hiding a gun. All of this, of course, culminated in Tanaka shouting “check out my gun case!” at the top of his lungs like the absolute buffoon he is.

        “Tanaka—you absolute buffoon!” I fumed, wishing I could be more upset than I actually was.

        “Sorry,” he atoned. It was hard to tell how sorry he was through his laugh.

        I tried to kick lightly at the back of his leg, but it was impossible to do well while speedwalking, so I gave up and decided that him getting some exercise in was a fair enough punishment.

        “Yeah, Tanaka.” Kyle snickered.

        “Get out of here Kyle—you clod,” Clera shouted behind us, as if to say “it’s your fault too!” Actually, she literally said that.

        I made the active effort to slow down and kick him—though, I put in less force than with Tanaka; Kyle was so much of a twig that I was worried I’d break a bone if I wasn’t careful. “Yeah! Return to the dust from whence you came!”

        My foot was surprised to find that his leg was pretty solid. Either that, or he has less muscle than I thought and I hit his bone directly.

        Even though we were trying to hurry, we were keeping our speed relatively low as a precaution against[b] Lindsy’s accident-prone nature.

        “How much time do we got?” I asked. Well, I was pretty out of breath from hauling around my bags, so it came out as more of a strained yell.

        Clera ruffled through her pockets for her phone. “UHHhhh…” It was hard to grab hold of anything with her skirt swinging around so much, so she slowed down to a walk. That walk then slowed to a stop, as she began to search more vigorously. I stopped. I could see her face paling.

        I slapped a hand on my forehead and pulled back my hair. Lindsy saw us stopping and thought we were taking a break.

        Kyle looked back confusedly. “What—what—why are we stopping?”

        “Clera lost her phone,” I groaned. Clera looked like she was about to cry—I think my tone of voice had done more damage than the phone itself. “It’s okay—you guys can go ahead—we’ll go back and search for it.”

        “Ah. Okay!” Without hesitation, Kyle began speeding off. After a few seconds, he spun around and came back. He made a face as if his conscience had gotten the better of him. He sped past us.

        “Where’re you going?”

        “It’ll be faster with the five of us.”

Clera still looked pitiful, and Kyle must’ve noticed, as he proceeded to snatch the cap from her head and ruffle her hair.

“Wha—” She looked up, her hands chasing after her hat instinctually.

Kyle fitted the cap onto his head and gave her a stupid grin. “I’ll give it back when we find your phone. Think of it as collateral.”

We spent the next few minutes retracing our steps. I checked out the lost and found, Lindsy and Tanaka checked with TSA (or, really, Tanaka checked with TSA, and Lindsy stood next to him), Clera tried looking around where we’d waited, and Kyle searched around the terminal entrances. Eventually, we regrouped, and we were all empty-handed.

I was trying my best not to complain—for Clera’s sake—but I was honestly feeling pretty upset, and it must’ve shown because Clera was looking worse than ever. Kyle’s mood had obviously dropped a bit, too.

“I’m gonna go use the restroom,” Lindsy voiced. If she were anyone else, I’d go with her, but whenever she stood, she could peer right over the stall walls, so it always felt a tad awkward being in the bathroom with her.

We sat at the benches, looking bummed out of our minds. I was sitting on a foldable chair, but I had slouched and slid so much that I was more off than on. On the other hand, Tanaka was laying sprawled out on the ground, dismaying over something entirely unrelated; he’d tried sitting in a chair, but it caused his skin to fold and bunch uncomfortably, so he’d resigned to the floor. Kyle was sitting against the wall, hunched over with his elbows purchased on his bony knees. The only one sitting up straight was Clera, which she always did when she was feeling guilty about something. She made a sniffing sound.

“I’m back.” Lindsy announced quietly.

My face was angled toward the ceiling, so I had to lift my head to look at her. “Welcome ba…” I stared at her for a few moments, waiting for my brain to decipher what had caught my attention. Clera’s phone! I shot up. “Where’d you find it?”

Everyone else looked over, each going through stages of confusion, realization, and elation one after another. Maybe it was because she spent so much time on the floor, or maybe it was because she spent so much time lost, but she’d discovered it slid under a decorative plant outside the restroom. How she even saw it with her height is beyond me.

Our celebration was short-lived, however, as when Clera turned on her phone, we found that the time on the lockscreen was thirty minutes after boarding time. The plane had left.

“UGGH!” I shouted. It was so loud and sudden—it surprised even me.

Whatever tears Clera had been holding in began gushing out. I guess I’d caught her off guard. She wasn’t sobbing or anything, but she couldn’t control the drops of water streaming down her cheeks and had little left to do than purse her lips and suppress her voice.

“Oh my gosh—no—sorry, it’s okay—it’s not your fault.” I hugged her from behind, stroking her forehead to try to calm her down.

Tanaka and Kyle looked away awkwardly. Kyle scratched the back of his head and found her cap again, so he removed it and offered it to Clera to cover her face. It was this last act of goodwill that really did Clera in. All of those embarrassing, gross sniffles and uneven breathing sounds she’d been keeping to herself came flooding out.

I sat us down and continued to hold her. She held her cap by the visor so the crown obscured her face, and she lifted her knees to make herself as small as physically possible, hoping it might prevent people from looking at her. I exhaled. Returning to reality, I made some phone calls to the school to explain what had happened. I got redirected and tossed around a few times, getting mixed reactions of sympathy and frustration from the people on the other end of the line.

Chapter 2: Where Are We Going?

“Mhm. Mhm. Okay. Thank you.” I got off the phone.

Clera had calmed down enough to talk, but her eyes and nose were still red. “What did they say?” She sniffed.

I smiled, raising my eyebrows and putting my smartphone away. “We’re in luck—looks like some doofus teacher overslept, so we’ll be joining him on the next flight—we can catch up with everyone else later.”

Her expression lightened. Kyle and Tanaka, who had started playing card games, burst into celebration, and Kyle used the opportunity to throw his cards into the air so he wouldn’t lose. Tanaka noticed and tackled him.

        “How long until the next flight?” Lindsy asked.

        “They said it shouldn’t be too long… in fact…”

        A phone rang.

I glanced over.

A disheveled man with glasses fished a phone out of his pocket, juggling it before getting a firm hold. “Hello?” He asked, a bit frazzled. “Uh-huh. I’m he—some kids? Ah… ah, got it.”

He looked around, and his gaze landed on us. He put the phone back to his ear and began walking over. “Yeah, yeah—I see them. Got it. No—no, I should be thanking you.”

He put his phone away. “Alright kiddos, I’ll be supervising you until we meet up with the rest of the group.”

“Oh,” I sat up, lifting Clera up and off of me. “Thank you—I’m Marin.”

“Ah,” he straightened the collar of his shirt. “You can call me Mr. Takaguchi. Takaguchi’s fine too. That’s what my students call me, at least.”

“Okay—great. Thanks, Mr. Takaguchi.”

“Yup…”

We stood awkwardly.

“So, what,  do we shake hands now, or…?”

I snorted. “Pf—I mean, we can?” I held a hand out palm up, amused at the proposition.

“Yeah,  maybe that’d dissolve all this weird tension.” He accepted my offer.

“So, when’s the next flight?”

“Right now.”

“Huh?”

“Yep—every buddy chop chop.” He clapped his hands together. “Let’s go—let’s go!”

Quickly, we gathered all of our belongings, rushing off towards our gate… which—we didn’t know where it was, so we just followed Mr. Takaguchi… who didn’t know where it was, so we asked a janitor for directions… And, everyone except Lindsy had to use the restroom, so…

Thankfully, despite all the delays, we made it aboard our flight, albeit within a hair’s breadth. Filing into our row, I plopped down into my seat, more mentally exhausted than anything. As I slid into position, Clera took my bag and handed it to Lindsy, who placed it in the overhead compartment. Kyle and Tanaka were conveniently in the row just in front of us, so I stole a bottle of lemonade they bought as compensation for our run-in with the TSA.

As the plane started down the runway, Lindsy popped some gum into her mouth to deal with the pressure. Clera took some too, though she wanted the candy more than she didn’t want decompression sickness. By the time Lindsy handed[c] one to me as well, the plane had begun its ascent. I was still drinking in my spoils of war, so I pocketed it for later.

        “… Where’re we going again?” Clera inquired between chewing sounds.

        I did a spit-take—I hadn’t drank a lot, but it triggered a disruptive fit of coughing that was really embarrassing. “You know about Obama mustard but not where we’re going on our field trip?”

        “Yyy… yyeeeeeeeeeesss?”

        “Yet, here you are—all amped up like a kid—how cute.” I pulled at her cheek, running a finger below her left eye. Her eyes were still puffy.

        “Yes, yes, I’m very cute and small,” she replied flatly. “So just tell me where we’re going already.” This wasn’t her first rodeo.

        “Come on, we’re going to…“ My mouth struggled for shapes, trying to sound out a word I’d forgotten.

        Her lips curled up into a mischievous smirk. “What’s this?” She nudged me with an elbow. “You don’t know where we’re going? After all of your excitement to tell me? How cute,” she teased, sarcasm overwhelming.

        “Shush—you’re contagious—what can I say?” I sighed. Must be nerves. “Lindsy, where’re we going?”

        Another consequence of her rapid growth was that Lindsy was always sleepy, a fact that was perfectly reflected in her droopy eyes and even better reflected in her closed eyes. They were closed. Seems outside the realm of what’s humanly possible, but alright. 

I kicked the back of Kyle’s chair.

“Yo,” I declared, waving a hand over the headrest. “Where’re we goin’?”

        “Huh? How do you not know?” Kyle mocked.

        “Quiet Kyle—nobody likes you,” I retorted, entirely kidding, “Tanaka, where’re we going?”

        “Where’re we going?”

        “Y’heard me.”

        “Yeah—give us the deets!” Clera shouted, trying her best to be seen over the seat.

        Tanaka laughed in his low voice. “Well, the place that we’re going is…” He slowed dramatically, and Kyle started up a drumroll with his hands.

        “Yes?” We urged.

        Kyle made a pop sound for the reveal.

        Tanaka opened his mouth.

        The suspense was killing us.

        “… Where are we going again?”

        Kyle cracked up, but then he saw that Tanaka was being serious. “Eh? For real dude? What’s up with y’all? Group amnesia? We’re goi…” He thought for a second. “Oh. Dang. Where are we going?”

        A shrill voice replied, “Heaven or hell.”

        HOLY—I whipped around. Mr. Takaguchi popped his head out from behind the seat, hands posed dubiously. He’d made his hair messy and pulled his glasses onto the tip of his nose; by his eyes, it looked like he hadn’t gotten a full-night’s sleep in years.

        “Mr. Takaguchi—you literally gave me a heart attack.” I grabbed my chest.

        Clera looked like she’d been shot, and even Lindsy had been frightened out of her slumber.

        “I have that effect on people. Kyle, you have an F in my class.”

        Kyle had a heart attack.

        “Anyways, what’re you guys on about?”

        “Right—yeah—“ I recollected myself, “where are we going?”

        “Oh.”

        A pause.

        “Oh.”

Another pause.

“Oh—wait!” His face lit up. “No. Dang it—brain fart—uh—it’s on the tip of my tongue.”

I laughed. “It’s on the boarding passes, isn’t it?”

“Yeah, but then I’ll be unsatisfied for the rest of the trip.”

“What’s this about?” Lindsy blinked groggily, rubbing her eye.

“Where we’re going.”

“Oh, it’s—”

Mr. Takaguchi cupped a hand over Lindsy’s mouth. “Wait.” An idea came to his mind. “Let’s all make a guess, and we’ll try to figure it out from context clues. Winner gets a free souvenir from everyone else.”

I eyed his face suspiciously. “You’ve already figured it out, haven’t you.”

“Nnnnooooooo.” He fanned at me dismissively.

I kept my eyes where they were.

“Look,” he shrugged. “Maybe I have—maybe I haven’t, but free stuff is free stuff, and I’m poor—please.” He clasped his hands together and made a begging gesture.

My eyebrows raised. “... Two free souvenirs. Three guesses to get it right.”

“Your los—I mean—deal.”

We grasped hands to sign the pact.

“Cambodia,” Tanaka declared.

I turned, “Is that… is that a common destination?”

“Yea, tons of tourist spots—what, you haven’t heard of the Angkor Wat? Phnom Penh?  Prasat Preah Vihear?”

“I haven’t… but alright,” I whipped out my phone, leaning over the back of their seats. “You’re gonna say Cambodia?”

He put a hand to his chin. “I change my mind—Dubai.”

“Final answer?”

“Mongolia.”

I typed his answers on an open note, taking another swig of lemonade. “Y’ didn’t have to spend your guesses in one place, y’ know.” I spoke, mouth half-full.

“Ah—” The realization dawned on him.

“Heh.” A smirk crept across my face.

“Did I just get conned?”

“I didn’t know you were such a travel buff.” I mused.

“That’s the only kind of ‘buff’ I am,” he pulled at his stomach fat.

“...”

“What’s wrong?”

I spit the lemonade back out into the bottle, half-coughing, half-laughing—wheezing and struggling for breath. “You can’t say those kinds of things when I’m drinking stuff.”

He eyed the updated contents of the bottle, disturbed. “Ew—gross.”

“Whatever—” I pulled it close to me, as if he were gonna take it away. “I wasn’t plannin’ on giving it back to you anyways.”

From behind us, I heard a soft giggling sound. Turning to look, I found Lindsy smothering her face into her bag, her hair drooped down. She was shaking, and her ears were visibly red where they showed. It was rare for anything to ever hit her humor sweet spot so dead-on.

She looked up from her stuff and glanced at Tanaka before breaking into another fit of quiet giggles.

“It wasn’t that funny.” I rubbed her back.

“Don’t listen to her—I’m hilarious.”

She tried again to straighten up, but it only took a few seconds of looking at him before she had to cup her face and lean back into yet another round of elated titters.

“Okie-dokie—well, while you’re settling down—Kyle, any bets?”

“Japan!” Kyle declared.

“We don’t know Japanese?”

“We don’t know Cambodian either—or any other country’s language, actually.”

“The U.K.? Canada?”

“They’re pretty much different languages.”

“Well, more reason to stay within the U.S.”

“I guess, but outside’s funner.”

“Fair,” I sat back down and turned to Clera, “what about you?”

“Uh… London?”

“Okay, I’ll guess New York, then.” I cocked my head back, “Mr. Takaguchi?”

He hoisted his head over the cushion so I could see his face.

“Yeah?”

“Your vote?”

“Paris.”

“Gotcha…”

All who’s left is Lindsy. 

She was recovering.

“Lindsy, where do you think we’re going?”

“Ah, no—” Her smile was audible. “I know already.”

“Oh, alright then—that’s everyone.” I put away the phone. “By the way, voting ends whenever we use up all our guesses or figure out the truth—but no looking it up.”

Now, we just have to pass the time until we arrive… We just have to…

 

Chapter 3: To Pass Time Until We Arrive

I tossed the gum from Lindsy into my mouth and began chewing to make myself busy. It was only now setting in that I really had no idea who these people were. We’d hardly ever talked to the boys—they were just those playful class clown-types that everyone knew for getting in trouble. And, of everyone there, it sounded like only Kyle had Mr. Takaguchi’s class. What did we even have in common? What were we talking about earlier?

Tanaka offered to let Lindsy touch his stomach fat. Lindsy made an excited “can I?” expression before delving right in and patting it. They seemed to be getting on fine.

“Uwah—so soft!” She exclaimed. I think she meant to keep this to herself since she quickly jolted back to judge his reaction.

“Isn’t it?” Kyle boasted. He rested his head atop Tanaka’s lap. “Whenever I’m stressed over school, his stomach seems to make all of my worries fly away.” He patted it twice for effect, making a low “doonk” sound.

Lindsy looked eager to resume her touching, so Kyle got out of the way.

I really wasn’t sure what I was watching, so I decided to ignore it for the time being.

“So, Mr. Takaguchi.”

“Mhm?” His head popped back over the seat. It was hard to tell if he was always laidback or just eternally sleep-deprived.

“What class do you teach?”

“Ah, Economics.”

“Oh, a numbers guy? That’s surprising”

“Nah, I suck at numbers, actually—letters are more my—what do you mean surprising?”

“Well, you kinda look like you’d be bad at math.”

“I mean, yeah—I look like a lot of things.” It seemed like an example came to mind. “Like, I said to call me Takaguchi—but that’s not actually my name.”

“Huh?”

“Yea—no. My name’s freakin’ Josué Calenmire, but like—I always eat tacos in class, and people think my fashion is outta wack, so, well, taco Gucci, tako guchi, Takaguchi… ‘cuz people think I look Japanese, but I’m actually fully Mexican.”

“PF—that’s so dumb.”

“Hey, what can I say—that’s my class. Only dumb people take it.”

“Isn’t Kyle in your class?”

“Case in point.”

“Wwwwwoooooooooowwwwwwww,” Kyle chimed in. “I heard that kumu.”

“Yeah, well, you gotta get your grades up before you can protest.”

“Kumu?” I asked dumbly.

“Hm?” Kyle scanned my face, trying to interpret what had me confused. “Oh—yeah—sorry—I was born in Hawaiʻi—old habit; kumu is ‘teacher’ in Hawaiian.”

“Oh, neat.”

“When did you move?” Clera inquired.

“Only ninth grade.”

“Tanaka too?” I asked.

“Hm? Why?”

“Oh, well, you two just sorta seem like a duo, so I figured you’d been together since you were children or something.”

He laughed. “No, we only met when I moved in next door.”

Tanaka used the opportunity to break away from Lindsy, since it was getting rather embarrassing for him. “He set me on fire when we first met.”

“Sorry?”

“SSHHHHHSJSSHH!” Kyle protested.

Tanaka ignored him. “He pressed the potato button on his microwave instead of the popcorn button and set his popcorn on fire, and when he went to take it out into the cold, I was coming over to greet our new neighbors, so he bumped into me and set my shirt on fire.”

“Look, in my defense—I’m not a reader—they both start with a P—they look the same.”

“See,” Mr. Takaguchi teased, “everyone in my class is dumb.”

“At least they aren’t short.”

“I’m not short! I’m average! Your class is just hella tall.”

A flight attendant came over to quiet us down, since we were bothering some of the other passengers. Somehow, it had already become night outside. Without Tanaka’s tummy as entertainment, Lindsy had fallen fast asleep, so we decided to follow suit.

The next morning, we arrived at the airport, so after filing off the plane and scouring for our luggage, we prepared to step outside.

“Alright, moment of truth guys.” I announced. “Time to find out where in the world we are.”

We braced ourselves, embarking on the trek up the steps towards the entrance. After a few debacles (Kyle dropped a coin, and it rolled all the way down the stairs), we finally reached the top. The light streaming in from the outside was blinding, but we ventured into it. As our eyes adjusted, we finally found ourselves in… the middle of the boonies. I turned to Lindsy, the keeper of secrets, for answers. She seemed to understand, as she opened her mouth to speak:

“Where are we?”
