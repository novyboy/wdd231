Creatures of a Liminal Space

I never thought I of all people would be starting a dream journal, but here goes nothing:

So, hello journal. Yesterday, I had a weird dream. I mean—everyone does, I know. A weird dream isn’t anything crazy—nothing out of the ordinary there. But, the dream keeps happening more and more often. So, I guess it would be more accurate to say that yesterday, I had a weird dream, again. It’s more lucid than your typical dream—realer—in the oddest sense of the word. I talked about it with Takahashi yesterday, and he suggested I write it down. I’m not one to believe that dreams have any deeper meaning, but hopefully this’ll take my mind off of them. Who knows, maybe I’ll even draw some conclusions about something. Oh, sorry—I forgot to date this.

January 1st, 2000

        When I woke up—that is to say—when I first came to… That is, in the dream—I found myself in the dark. Looking around, my eyes were drawn to a bridge of sorts, crossing some chasm that dropped off into an abyss. It was less of a bridge, really—more of a rectangular beam, jutting diagonally from one black wall to another… I feel like that’s still the wrong description. How’s this? Imagine an unlit hallway. Now imagine it without the walls, but it still keeps the darkness in. If you were to then look at that hallway from the outside, that’s what the path looked like. And, I said “walls”—they were more like the sides of a ravine—of a fissure—only man-made. At least, not natural. Not horribly interesting, I know, but it was just about the only scenery for miles, so I feel it important to get right. Speaking of, even though there were no discernible obstructions, I couldn’t see the horizon. Maybe because of fog, maybe because there wasn’t a horizon in this dream, or maybe because despite the apparent vastness of the area, the place was in reality entirely enclosed. Regardless, I couldn’t see the horizon.

Anyways, checking beneath me, I found that I was standing on the same kind of structure that I saw in the distance. Even up close, its material remained a mystery, but something within me told me that it was indestructible—that even if it were needle-thin it would support my weight. As that impression rested in my mind, I looked back to the path. Only now there was a figure there. The figure was hooded—cloaked in black robes. I couldn’t tell if it was human or if it had a face, but I could tell it knew I was there. Noiselessly, the form raised its hands up and clapped them together loudly. A pause, from both of us, as the noise was absorbed into the space. Then, it began to clap again and again, more loudly, until it was almost as if it was applauding.

I glanced away for less than a second and all at once it was right before me. Looming over me, it clapped one last time, and as its hands met, I found myself back on my bed at home, having woken up from my dream before the clap could sound.
