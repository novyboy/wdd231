Chapter 1 - Birds in a Cage

.1 - The Expression

Have you ever seen a bear in a pen?

No? Well—no, yeah—I guess that’s not the most common sight in the world.

Umm… How about—oh! A lion—duh—obviously. Or a… gorilla, maybe?

Maybe those examples are a bit exotic, how about something smaller—like a bird?  In any case, if you’ve happened upon that kind of opportunity like I have, whether as an occupation or a study of some kind—or just by chance, maybe you’ve noticed that there’s something they all seem to have in common. The expression—not necessarily sad or angry, but… understanding—a look of deep knowing—knowing on some level, whether consciously or subconsciously, that they’re in a cage. So… why? Why does this girl have that same look in her eyes?

*        *        *

Stirring, I recede further into my blanket.
        A phone buzzes and beeps away in the background, vibrations permeating through the pillow yanked over my ears. The one time I want my phone to die, it’s at full power—either because I plugged it in to charge yesterday for this exact reason or because it’s beeping was a new technology built to drain and sap energy from me. Excruciating—so probably the latter. As the noise becomes unbearable, my arm fights to unearth itself—emerging from beneath the protective warmth of my sheets to stretch and probe for the sound source in haphazard attempts to stop it while maintaining the orientation of my body. A few miscalculations and some desperate prodding later, I finally seize hold of the cell phone and shut off the alarm.

Quiet at last, I retreat comfortably into the puddle-shaped territory that I sprawled lazily across and claimed last night.

And, just in time for my backup alarm, too. In my haste to return to the land of dreams, I somehow succeeded in misplacing my phone, so I’m forced to pull away my bedding to better my odds of ever finding it again. At this point, I have no choice but to accept the fact that I won’t be getting back to sleep, so I wipe the morning tears from my eyes and slowly begin to remember why I bought a screaming demon slab in the first place. Picking up the near-discarded device, I take a cursory glance at the screen before performing a proper scan through half-open eyes, trying my best to derive some meaning from the hazy characters. There’s an admittedly long delay before I realize not only that, one, it’s a Friday rather than the weekend I was hoping for (Mondays and Tuesdays are my days off, but I have an extra hour to sleep in on Saturdays and Sundays), but also that, two, that wasn’t my backup alarm, but my backup backup alarm’s first cousin twice removed—leaving me with about zero seconds flat to get ready for work.

Launching into a frenzied panic, I clatter together my belongings and get dressed, cautiously sidestepping about my obstacle course of a room as I work the fine balance of grabbing things I need and being decent enough before I reach the door.

By 7:30, I scramble out the door, scarfing down bits of breakfast between the exchange of “good morning” greetings with the aunty next unit over. It’s unclear whether she can differentiate my chews from my responses or if she just bases her reactions off of context clues. Though, I wager that she succeeds in neither, given how she decides now to be a good time for a gracious donation of leftovers. Without the necessary time-expenditures or coordination to fumble the lock open, I rush down the apartment stairwell, just about throwing the gift into my bike basket, and just about sending my bike flying. With a lazy push of one digit combo change, I yank off my bike lock and toss it in by the food—straddling aboard my seat and kicking myself into motion. A few times.

Licking a few crumbs off my finger, I grip the handlebars firmly and begin flying down the street. An out-of-place tie flops in and out of view as I speed around a turn, signalling to me that I might have dressed a little too formal. Oh well, no time to turn back now.

And, there really wasn’t, as not even a second after checking in with the kind man at the entrance of the zoo, the hour hand of the gate clock ticks up. Relieved, I park my bike and walk to the dressing room to dawn the staff uniform, storing away the flippity-floppity tie with a twinge of embarrassment. From there, I patrol around, performing the perfunctory checkups I do every day to make sure everyone’s nice and healthy.

The crowds come filing in at around the same time I finish up with our token tiger.

“Alright Tubo, looks like you’re ready for a busy day.”

“Tubo” is the nickname I gave the tiger for its comically stumpy demeanor that seems to contrast its self-important attitude—as you may or may not be able to tell, I’m quite the expert when it comes to names.

As if carried in by the crowds, the scorch of the sun starts setting in, prompting me to adjust the staff-branded visor to better shield my face. Noon rolls around, and the weather’s particularly muggy, forming beads of sweat that roll past my temples. My back and my armpits are beginning to feel like a suitable place for our tropical dart frogs, and I’m starting to worry that it might be as obvious to others as it is to me. Pulling uncomfortably at the fabric clinging to my skin, I decide to distract myself by tending to the animals in the petting area.

YOUCH!

Putting a hand to my back, I turn to face what—or rather, who—just hit me. Judging by the self-amused laughter, I have a guess.

“Morning Jessa!” the man says. His slapping hand stays hovering at the end of its arc of motion. That is Daniel Bowens, a rambunctious coworker of mine who never seems to get it into his head that I’d prefer a gentler greeting.

“Hello Daniel,” I reply sassily, “Rowdy as ever, I see. Also, it’s noon.”

“How about that! And hello to you, too: Rodney, Jericho, Phillip!” He greets the rabbits.

It’s Rebecca, Joshua, and Lily, I correct in my head, still rubbing the stinging sensation away.

“It’s Rebecca, Joshua, and Lily.”

Oh.

“I was close enough, wasn’t I?”

The rabbits stare blankly, the bushy fur above their eyes furrowing in disagreement.

I can’t help but smile a bit. “Not remotely, especially with that last one. Phillip?”

He squishes their cheeks together. “Well, I got the ‘li’ in there, didn't I?”

Since he can never remember the nicknames that I give them, and he can’t remember the names he gives them, Daniel is the type to make up a new one every time he refers to them.

“I… suppose? Better than your last few attempts.”

“What, Douglass, Erin, and Poe?”

“N—no? Actually, it was Walter, Chloe, and Denice.”

He let out a bellowing laugh, “I know, I know—I’m just messing with you.”

Unlikely.

Rising to my feet, I begin walking over to the next station. He trails behind.

“I thought you had something to do.” I smirk.

“Me? No, I’m on break right now, so I thought I’d keep you company,” he responds charmingly, leaning one arm on a post.

Glancing back, I spin on my heel to face him before turning forward again with a smile.

“Well! That’s very kind of you to offer, but I’m quite alright.”

“Wha—” His voice cracks a bit, so he clears his throat and places a hand on his Adam’s apple as if to steady it. “WhaAAT? N—no, NAhh, I really think you could use a hand. My hand. Me.”

He embellishes his speech with a slidy inflection and awkward movement to play off his little vocal mishap.

I chuckle. “If you insist.”

“I do,” he says with a raise of the finger. “Insist. I do insist.”

His finger retracts slowly until it curls back into a fist. He presses his lips together, which seems to indicate that he’s questioning his own intelligence. A rare sight.

Hoisting a box of various fruits up, I saunter over to him and drop the box into his arms while he’s still lost in thought, causing him to nearly fall over from the sudden shift in weight.

“Right—if you’re gonna follow me around anyways, you might as well actually make yourself useful.”

Walking back over to where I got the other, I pick up an additional box of fruit while listening to discombobulated grunts and jostling sounds behind me.

“C’mon, what’s all that muscle for?” I tease.

Finally stabilized, he retorts: “Hey, I just wasn’t ready for it, that’s all.”

“Oh good, I guess that means you can handle another box, then!”

Before giving him a chance to respond, I push a second crate onto the other. This time, he seems much better situated to receive more weight, but he still dips slightly to readjust.

“See?” He declares proudly. “No problem.”

“Huh, whad’ya know?”

Picking up a third, final box, I lead the way to our star attraction, stepping through the employee entrance and placing down the produce by a large bowl.

“Hey, so, I was wondering—” Daniel situates the other two next to mine before turning them on their sides to pour them into the bowl. A sneaky gray appendage reaches for an apple perched atop my stack.

“Not so fast, Sweeney,” I scold, gently brushing the trunk aside. “Wait until it’s in the bowl.”

Sweeney, our elephant, gains his name from the ever-mischievous look in his eyes and the wrinkles that make him look like he’s smirking 24/7, wise with age—playful at heart.

Moving away as if he’d anticipated my loving reprimand from the beginning, he rustles my hair and steals Daniel’s visor to greet him.

Daniel looks up with the kind of bright, charmed smile that shouts, “HEY!”

Sweeney always reminds me of Daniel, and perhaps as a testament to that, they always seem to get along.

“Now, now, no need to grasp for attention, big buddy,” he replies, rubbing the taunting trunk to persuade a return. This doesn’t work. Instead, the hat finds a new home right on the elephant’s head.

“It suits him,” I muse.

“Aww c’mon man, you can’t do this on the hottest day of the century!” He mopes.

Releasing a small chortle, I pull off my cap and yank it down over his eyes. “Here! Don’t be such a baby.”

A loud, snickering trumpet roars above us, and Sweeney reaches for another helping of fruit. In the short time we’ve been there, he’s already snuck four or five trunkfuls without me noticing. I can’t reasonably get angry since he is taking from the bowl, like I told him to, so I relent.

“Alright,” I announce, standing, "I’m just gonna check in with the birds—then, I’m on break. Don’t get fat, Sweeney!”

Shooting a quick goodbye to our token troublemaker, Daniel jogs over to catch up to me before slowing to match my stride.

“So, how come you never hang out with me during your breaks?” He inquires innocently.

“Because then it wouldn’t be a break, now, would it?”

“Wh—HEY!”

“Joking, joking. The better question is how you keep it up: walking all day without pause.”

“My secret is that I take two breaks.” He grins, flashing a peace sign as either a sign of victory or as a way to emphasize the two.

“Captain Boss will get mad.”

“Captain Boss” is neither a captain nor our boss, but he’s one of our coworkers whose time serving in the military really reflects in his merciless work ethic and his rectangular glasses. Along with his tidy but menacing appearance, he’s a stickler for details. Our real boss is a rather laidback, round man with a bald spot that gleams in the sun.

“It’s okay. I still do the work I’m assigned, and I’ll be taking it to the grave, so he’ll never find out.”

“Not if I tell him.”

“I’ll take you to the grave as well, if I have to.”

“And what, be crammed in the same plot as you? No way.”[a]

I smirk defiantly, facing forward to continue walking.

Daniel seems to remember a thought from earlier, so he opens his mouth to pick it up, “So, I was wondering—if, this Saturday, maybe—I mean, if it’s alright with you—”

The squawks and cheeps of a dozen parrots cut him short, signifying that we’ve entered the range of the aviaries. Pacing around to the side of the bird exhibit, I open a step ladder to access one of the cages.

“So—” He makes an attempt at speaking over them, but an additional screech dampens his enthusiasm, and he decides to save it for later. Or, then again, he decides last moment to have another go: “He—”

“BWWAAAAAAAAAAHHHHHH!”

A small commotion behind us. Turning, I find a little boy and a middle school girl on the ground. I step down from my ladder and hurry over.

“What happened?” I ask the bawling toddler, running to kneel next to him, somehow expecting more than a blubbery mess despite my experience caring for kids.

“BWWWAAAAAHHHHHH—WAAAAA—WWAAAAAAAAAAAAH~”.

“He was running too fast and bumped into this girl here,” says his mother, already hoisting him over her shoulder and in the middle of quieting him down.

“He’s alright—more sad about the ice cream, really—but her…” She signals to the girl on the ground as she cradles the wailing boy between her arms. The girl’s been the victim to not only a badly skinned knee, but her skirt’s been marred by the half-eaten popsicle the boy had dropped in the aftermath.

Focusing my attention to her, I remove a handkerchief from my back pocket and some disinfectant from a fanny pack wrapped around my shoulder. The past few years have taught me to always have those supplies on standby, since scrapes tended to be the most common symptom of the average zoo-goer. To clear the way for others, I have Daniel carry her over to a nearby bench beneath the shade.

“Sorry, this is going to hurt a bit,” I recite automatically while dabbing the handkerchief with alcohol.

As I press the cloth against her injury, she grimaces instinctively, the muscles on her leg tensing and flinching before easing up. Carefully, I remove the specks of asphalt that’ve clung to her wound, cleaning the dirt out as I go. Thank goodness she’s an older kid, or she’d be bawling her eyes out, I think, impressed by how well-behaved she was. Extracting the last few pieces, I look up at her for the first time with a beaming grin intended to lighten the mood: “Finished! That wasn’t so bad, was it?”

As the intensity of the smile clears from my eyes, I look into hers.

I shudder, a cold chill running down my spine. Behind me, the parrots and cockatoos screech, clamoring their wings at the bars of the cage in what seems a desperate attempt to escape. Escape? Escape what? Emptiness—a sedated abyss reflects before me that seems to stretch on for miles and miles—on and on and on and on and…

I blink. There’s no noise behind me. All of the birds are perched calmly on their posts. A double take suggests that the absence I just saw in her eyes was imagined, and she’s beginning to look a bit embarrassed by my staring. Quickly, I stand, and, looking at her clothes, I remember that she’s suffered not one, but two incidents.

I smile again. “Let’s get you changed, shall we?”

Handing her some free tourist apparel, merchandise that would usually overcharge a good $20-40, we lend her the break room to change. Breathing a deep sigh, I adjust my cap and blow back the loose strands of hair in my face. Amber light filters through the window and draws my attention to the table. Upright, I plant my feet on the ground and lean over to get a better look. Where the sunlight hits the table, there’s a sticky note and a few crumpled bills weighed down by some spare coins. The note reads:

“Don’t even try taking your bike home! Use this money to pay for a taxi.”

Next to the shockingly neat handwriting, a reprimanding doodle.

I ignore him.

He stole my bike.

H—he stole my BIKE! Where my bike should be parked is yet another note, reinforced to the public bike rack with extra pieces of tape:

“Literally what did I just say dude?

ps. you should really lock your bike properly”

Rude. Warranted, but rude.

Out of options to rebel, I have little choice but to be obedient and call up a taxi. I do check one of the big clocks, positioned every few meters, to see if I have the time and patience to trek over to wherever Daniel is, but the taxi arrives first, so I guess he’s lucky.

Home at last, I fiddle with the lock and wrench off my shoes, leaving them messy by the door. Staggering down the entryway, I throw myself onto the couch. For a second, I just lay there, facedown against the leather cushions. Then, channeling all of my energy, I unsuction my face to turn it to the side. As I stare into space, the remote on the table catches my unsmooshed eye, so I flick on the old TV the previous owner of the room left behind, but…

“... ‘ay, look at me— (WHO) dancin’— I dunno what I’ll be chancin’ bu—”

Skip.

“... Braaandon, did you eat my pomegranate?”

“Maybe...?”

“Oh you—”

A laugh track begins, so I switch channels.

“... In more recent news, the ‘sleeping disease’ is up another—”

As I thought, it doesn’t suit me.

“Ughhhhh. Alright! I get it! I’ll go to sleep!”

Shutting off the TV, I toss the remote onto the floor.

Reluctantly, I close my eyes, shifting and turning until my body determines it’s comfortable enough to stop bothering me.

.1 - A Memory

“It happened two summers ago.”

“I just got home from soccer practice as the sun began to sink beneath the horizon. After studying at my desk for about an hour, I decided to shower and brush my teeth before heading to sleep.”

“But as I hopped into bed, I realized I left my phone on the bathroom counter, so I went back to get it. Peering inside, I found my phone exactly where I left it, but as I reached in to grab it, I noticed that the mirror was all fogged up, so like any decent person, I tried to wipe the fog away using the bottom of my hand. Wiping and wiping, it was as if I was doing nothing. Then I realized—everything beside me had cleared up, and the only thing still blurry was my figure, staring blankly at me from behind the glass.”

A flash of lightning is met with synchronized screams from her audience.

She snickers, self-amused. “Well, turns out it was just a really stubborn spot—I tried using some of my dad’s shaving cream, and it worked like a charm,” she reveals. As she does, both her hands go up, uncovering a thunderous phone which controls a bright light near the window.

Yumi, the one who screamed the loudest (she always does), hits Madison lightly on the arm, sending her into stitches. Yumi’s real name is Yumehara, but she apparently doesn’t like that it “sounds like a last name”, so she chooses to go by “Yume” instead. Only, after moving from Japan, there’s really no one who can pronounce it properly, so “Yumi” is the current heir to the throne as natural “next-in-line.

Madison Hazing, on the other hand, who’s still pleased with her resounding success, is an absolute tomboy who constantly exudes the most extreme levels of chaos.

“Hey! You girls go to sleep now y’hear?” Madison's mom shouts from downstairs.

Instantly, we quiet down, but Madison clearly has no intention of listening, as not only a second passes before she grins impishly, causing her lax, troublemaker eyelids to nearly come together: “So? Who wants to go next?”

Yumi, not one to defy adults, dismisses her. “You wouldn’t if that was Kailana’s mom.”

“That's because Kailana’s mom is scary!” She counters.

“Hoh yup, my mom is very scary,” Kailana chimes.

Kailana Cientero, owner of the other shriek, is a mixture of Hawaiian, Spanish, and an excess of other ethnicities and cultures. One can never really get a read on her or her accent.

“Well, I'm going to sleep.”

“Awww, boo. Lame.”

Kailana yawns. “Yeah me too, I like sleep.”

“Maan, you too?” Madison laments. “Aaalright, lights out, then.”

Tugging on the pull chain, Madison turns off the dim ceiling lamp while everyone pulls up their covers. As much as Madison hates doing what she’s told, she also hates doing things alone. But if she can find a way to cause trouble while following the rules, she will. A few minutes pass before a whisper pierces the silence.

 “Guess what?” Madison peeps.

“What?” Kailana echoes back.

“Chicken butt.”

The joke receives some undeserved giggles before Yumi shushes her.

Madison continues. “Guess who?”

“Who?” Kailana asks in spite of herself.

“Chicken poo.”

Kailana snorts, causing Madison to have to suppress a laugh. Despite reprimanding earlier, Yumi fails to maintain her dignity and starts to shake. Loopy from a lack of sleep, I can’t help but chuckle, too.

Regaining her composure, Yumi musters the seriousness to shush her again, nearly breaking into a laugh at the tail end.

A decent span of silence passes.

“Guess why?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“NO!”

“Chicken pie.”

“Gosh.” I cringe disapprovingly as everyone breaks into another fit of hysteria, and I soon join them.

“GO. TO SLEEP! How many times do I hafta tell y’all?” The door flings open abruptly to reveal an irate mom, so we all scramble beneath the blankets in a futile effort to feign innocence. Satisfied with her brief display of authority, she shuts the door.

In less than a heartbeat, we forget the fear she instilled in us and begin giggling again, so Madison’s mom swings the door against the wall and roars like a dinosaur in a cross of frustration and playfulness.

Finally to bed, Yumi’s snores fill the room, and Kailana mutters into her pillow, Madison sprawls across the both of them. Sleepovers with them are always risky since you’re more likely to be “over” than "to sleep”.

Thankfully, after a bit of waiting, I drift off.

.2 - A Dream

It happened two summers ago.

I open my eyes and find it still dark.

No... No, that’s not right. A mysterious weight presses against the bridge of my nose. Grasping for it, my fingers are met with a plasticy sensation. Pressing against and feeling around some protrusions, I identify whatever it is to be some kind of clunky piece of hardware strapped around the back of my head.

After a few attempts that fail at removing it, I fish it off and throw it against the floor with a grunt, causing a loud clatter and some ripping noises. My vision now unobscured, I discover myself in a sterile white room. An indistinct, yet intense light flashes on, forcing me to shield my eyes. Still, the light seems to find a way to me—the exact location of the source almost indiscernible aside from its vague upward direction.

Sitting up, I figure out what had generated the tearing sound. Where I’ve been laying my head, a bundle of wires gather into a flat, transparent node, the straight, carbon covering stripped away. Moving to stand, I feel a sharp tug on my wrist, and I glance down to find a loose square of what seems to be paper, which once secured a thin tube to my arm, now dangling. Tracing the cord with my eyes, I find that it feeds back into the bed.

The entrance, which barely stands out from the rest of the wall, slides open, and two men step in.

“Oo—OOOooooooOoo! We are aliens from the planet Zorg!” One begins.

“We are come for cows and canoodles!” Declares the other.

“No—Phil—that’s indecent!”

“What!—what is?”

“Canoodles!”

“Wh—I didn't know that was a real word!” He retorts. “Ah—er—WE ARE COME FOR COWS AND CABOODLES.”

“Well—well, no, but I thought you should know since you’re going through the trouble of changing it.”

“Oh. Well, thanks Tom.”

I stare at them blankly. Neither of them seem particularly alien-like. In fact, both are very much shaped like regular humans. Then again, neither of them seem very normal. If there’s anything alien about them, it’s their personalities.

Perhaps sensing my doubt, they continue.

“Halt, Zeric!” The one I assume is Tom commands.

“You’ll never catch me alive!” Hollers the other who’s probably Phil, suddenly sporting a ridiculous mustache.

What... What in the world is this?

“Kagerou!!!” Phil shouts passionately.

The only feasible explanation I can come up with is that I’m dreaming. After all, dreams invent the sort of things that even bad authors don’t have tolerance for. So, without saying anything at all (I’m not about to ask my dream for permission), I lay back down and shut my eyes.

.3 - An Unfamiliar Familiar

Rustling under my sheets, I struggle again to shut off the blaring alarm. Thankfully, it’s Saturday, so...

…

…

…It’s Friday? 

I’m awake now—and staring at my phone screen, it’s clear as day: June 16, 2017, Friday.

Gosh, I really can't keep my days straight, can I? Resigning, I accept my fate and begin preparing for the day, not wanting a repeat of yesterday's morning rush. And, this time, I won’t have to deal with my eye-catching tie. Jeans and a blank t-shirt will be just fine.

Whipping up a quick, easy sandwich, I walk out the door, phone in ha—phone in han... phone in…

What? 

The phone, squared in the palm of my right hand so I could put away my lip balm, is gone. Taking a better look, so’s my lip balm! And… my handbag. And, the sandwich that was hanging so precariously from my mouth, and my—My CLOTHES! 

Instead of the oh-so-comfortable jean-tee combo I walked out with, I find a rather unwieldy lab coat pinned with an ID and long, stark-black pants. Yet, more shocking is the no next-door neighbor, no apartment stairwell, no apartment that greets me. In the place of the worn building with its paint flakes and aged nameplates is a polished white hallway, modern and with sleek, dual-tinted walls.

What is happening?

“Oh, Emma, good, you’re up.”

Did I fall asleep in the wrong house? Am I gonna get arrested for trespassing?

“You may still be adjusting to life here, but I’d appreciate it if you tried not to cause so much trouble for others?”
